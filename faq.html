Overview

General Questions

EXP EQUITY is a mutual-aid professional skills exchange that connects recent graduates with businesses through escrow-backed micro-gigs. Graduates earn real portfolio credits and get their essential living costs paid directly — rent, utilities, groceries, and transport — without touching raw cash.

It addresses the entry-level catch-22: you need experience to get a job, but you need a job to pay your bills. EXP EQUITY breaks that cycle by letting you trade real skills for verified work history and direct bill payments.

There are two roles on the platform:

Graduate Contractors — recent graduates from accredited institutions (or holders of recognized industry certifications, or self-taught developers with a sandbox track record) who offer technical, business, creative, or in-person services.

Sponsors — commercial entities, small business owners, solopreneurs, or individual alumni who fund micro-gigs in exchange for deliverables.

Three structural differences separate EXP EQUITY from freelance platforms:

1. Isolated talent pool. Upwork pits U.S. graduates against global contractors at unsustainable rates. EXP EQUITY restricts the applicant pool to un-established emerging professionals, making it an "impact sponsorship" rather than a commodity procurement.

2. Restricted payout. Earnings don't go to your bank account — they go directly to your landlord, utility company, and grocery store. This gives sponsors complete transparency and eliminates financial mismanagement risk.

3. Scope-locked contracts. Once a sponsor funds escrow, the Definition of Done checklist is legally frozen. No hidden requests, no scope creep, and a 72-hour auto-approval window protects you from being ghosted.

All gigs must fall under one of four authorized verticals:

💻 Technical & Software — Web dev, script automation, front-end layout, data parsing, database setup, API integration.

📋 Business Administration — Data entry, inbox triage, meeting coordination, spreadsheet auditing, market research.

🎨 Creative & Design — Graphic design, branding, UI/UX, document design, social assets, video editing.

🤝 On-Site / In-Person — Local physical operations: event setup, inventory, moving assistance, manual labor support.

Getting Started

Onboarding & Verification

EXP EQUITY uses a three-path verification model to validate contractors without creating unnecessary systemic barriers:

Path A

Academic Credentialing

Upload a transcript or diploma from an accredited institution.

Path B

Industry Certification

Upload recognized certs (e.g., Google Professional Certificates, AWS Certifications, State Vocational Licenses).

Path C

Sandbox Proof-of-Work

Register as Level 1. Complete 3 micro-gigs (max $50 each) with 5-star ratings to auto-upgrade to Verified.

Sponsors complete three steps: (1) create an account with organizational details (business name, industry, website) or individual capacity; (2) connect a primary funding source via the integrated Stripe payment processor; (3) pass a pre-authorization check — a micro-authorization ($0.00 or $1.00 hold) to verify the financial instrument is valid before any real funds are committed.

For self-taught developers and creators without formal credentials, the Sandbox track lets you prove your skills through work rather than paper. Level 1 accounts are restricted to low-risk, low-value micro-gigs (max $50 per gig). After completing three projects with 5-star ratings, the system automatically upgrades your account to "Verified," unlocking the full gig board.

Project Process

Gig Lifecycle & Escrow

Every contract on the platform progresses linearly through five explicit states:

Pending → Active (Escrow Locked) → Submitted → Paid / Settled

In the event of a conflict, a project enters Disputed (Escrow Frozen) status instead of Paid.

The DoD is an immutable, objective checklist of requirements set by the Sponsor before any work begins. It must contain a minimum of three binary (Yes/No) metrics — e.g., "Must be deployed to a live URL," "Must be 1,000 words," "Must use brand hex codes."

Once the Sponsor funds escrow and the project shifts to Active, the DoD is legally frozen. The Sponsor cannot add or change requirements after this point. All dispute arbitration is judged strictly against this original checklist.

When you submit deliverables, the project status shifts to Submitted and the Sponsor receives an automated notification. They have exactly 72 hours to review the work against the DoD checklist.

If the Sponsor does not respond within 72 hours, the platform automatically approves the work and releases escrow funds to your Restricted Wallet. This prevents you from being ghosted or held hostage by an unresponsive client.

Contracts are bounded by a mandatory maximum of two (2) revision cycles. A revision round is defined as a specific list of modifications requested to correct discrepancies between the submitted work and the original DoD checklist.

Requests for aesthetic overhauls that diverge from the original brief are not considered valid revisions and are rejected by the system as unauthorized scope creep. This is enforced at the admin level.

The platform enforces a mandatory liquidity protocol to prevent capital stagnation:

If a project stays in Pending for 48 hours without a bid, the platform automatically requests that the Sponsor increase the budget (based on category benchmarking data).

If it remains unclaimed for 7 consecutive calendar days, the project is automatically cancelled and the full escrowed amount is immediately refunded to the Sponsor's original payment method.

Payouts

The Restricted Digital Wallet

The Zero-Cash-Touch Policy is the core integrity mechanism of EXP EQUITY. Under no circumstances is unrestricted cash disbursed directly from a Sponsor to a Contractor's personal bank account.

This policy exists to eliminate fraud, give Sponsors complete payout transparency, and protect Contractors from financial mismanagement pressure. All capital introduced by a Sponsor moves directly into the platform's Escrow Account via Stripe before any work begins.

Wallet disbursements are locked to verified essential categories via Merchant Category Codes (MCCs):

🏠 Housing / Rent — Direct electronic transfer to verified corporate property management portals or commercial landlords.
⚡ Utilities — Electric, gas, water, municipal wastewater, and internet service providers.
🛒 Groceries — Authorized transactions at physical or digital supermarkets.
🚌 Transportation — Commercial fueling stations or municipal public transit authorities.

Any attempt to spend at unauthorized categories (e.g., alcohol retailers, luxury goods, gift cards) is automatically blocked by the system.

1. Ledger Crediting — Upon Sponsor approval (or 72-hour auto-approval), Escrow funds transfer to your Restricted Digital Wallet. This is a ledger update, not a cash transfer to your bank.

2. Request Generation — You navigate to your Wallet dashboard and select an audited Vendor Endpoint (e.g., "Pay $150 to my registered water bill").

3. Balance Verification — The system checks you have sufficient Ledger Equity to cover the request.

4. Direct B2B Transfer — The platform initiates an ACH, virtual card transaction, or manual check routing directly to the corporate vendor. During the MVP phase, the Admin manually verifies receipts before executing direct-to-vendor payment.

5. Receipt Logging — Both you and the system log the transaction. Your ledger is debited by the exact payout amount.

Conflict Resolution

Disputes & Arbitration

If you believe the submitted work does not comply with the DoD checklist, you must trigger a formal Disputed state within the 72-hour review window. Failure to act within this window results in automatic escrow release to the Contractor — you cannot file a dispute after auto-approval.

Once disputed, the Escrow Account is frozen by the backend and the Admin assumes the role of a neutral arbitrator.

The arbitration procedure is structured and time-bounded:

Step 1 (48 hrs): The Sponsor submits written documentation specifying exactly which DoD metrics were not fulfilled.

Step 2 (48 hrs): The Contractor submits counter-documentation proving checklist compliance, or demonstrating that the rejection constitutes unauthorized scope creep.

Step 3: The Admin audits the deliverables strictly against the initial DoD. Subjective arguments about personal taste, market performance, or shifting corporate preferences are completely disregarded.

There are two binding, final outcomes:

Judgment for the Sponsor: If the Admin determines the Contractor failed to satisfy the objective DoD metrics, the full escrow amount is returned to the Sponsor. The Contractor receives zero allocation and a Strike is logged against their account.

Judgment for the Contractor: If the Admin determines the Contractor met the checklist, or that the Sponsor's rejection was structurally invalid (i.e., scope creep), the full escrow amount is released immediately to the Contractor's Restricted Wallet.
